# CODE OF CONDUCT

## Our Commitment

CodexClava is built around a simple idea:

> Build what you wish existed.

We believe the best communities are built by people who are willing to experiment, learn, question assumptions, share what they know, and build together.

Everyone should be able to participate, contribute, and learn here without harrasment, discrimination, intimidation, or gatekeeping.

## Our Standards

Members and contributors are expected to:

* **Be respectful.** Treat people with dignity, even when you strongly disagree.
* **Challenge ideas, not people.** Tehnical debate is encouraged. Personal attacks are not.
* **Be curious.** Ask questions, explain your reasoning, and help others learn.
* **Give credit.** Recognize the work, ideas, and contributions of others.
* **Be honest.** Clearly distinguish facts, assumptions, experiments, opinions, and uncertainty.
* **Own mistake.** Everyone gets things wrong. Acknowledging and correcting mistakes is part of building well.
* **Welcome newcomers.** Don't use knowledge, experience, or jargon as a weapon or a gatekeeping tool.
* **Build in good faith.** Contributions should be made with the intention of improving the project or community.
* **Respect boundaries and privacy.** Do not expose personal information or share private conversations without permission.
* **Leave things better than you found them.** This applies to code, documentation, discussions, issues, and the community itself.

## Unacceptable Behavior

The following behavior is not tolerated:

* Harrassment, bullying, threats, or intimidation.
* Discrimination or derogatory behavior based on personal characteristics or identity.
* Personal attacks, insults, or deliberately humiliating others.
* Sexual harrasment or unwelcome sexual attention.
* Doxxing or sharing someone's private or sensitive information without consent.
* Trolling, baiting, or delibrately provoking conflict.
* Repeatedly disrupting discussions or collaboration in bad faith.
* Plagiarism or deliberately misrepresenting someone else's work as your own.
* Retaliating against somewho raises a legitimate concern.
* Malicious use of community resources, infrastructure, or projects.
* Publishing security vulnerabilities or private information in a way that unnecessarily puts people or systems at risk.
* Any other conduct that a reasonable person would consider inappropriate or harmful in a professional technical community.

## Disagreement Is Welcome

CodexClava is not a place where everyone has to agree.

You are allowed to say:

* "I think this approach is wrong."
* "This design has a serious flaw."
* "I don't understand the reasoning here."
* "I disagree with the maintainer."
* "We should try something completely different."

Strong disagreement can produce better work.

The boundary is **how** that disagreement is expressed. Critism should be directed towards the work, reasoning, implementation, or proposal. It should not be directed towards someone's dignity or identity.

Being technically correct does not give anyone permission to be abusive.

## For Maintainers

Maintainers have additional responsibility.

Maintainers should:

* Apply community standards consistently.
* Avoid using project authority to silence reasonable critism.
* Explain important decisions when practical.
* Be open to reconsidering decisions when presented with new information.
* Handle reports confidentially where appropriate.
* Avoid conflicts of interest and disclose them when relevant.
* Remember that being a maintainer is a responsibility, not a status symbol.

Maintainers are not exempt from this Code of Conduct.

## Security and Responsibible Disclosure

If you discover a security vulnerability, do not use public issues, pull requests, discussions, or other public channels to expose sensitive details before there is an opportunity to address the issue.

Report security concerns privately through the project's designated security reporting channel.

Do not use a vulnerability to harm users, access information that does not belong to you, or disrupt services.

Responsible security research and good faith disclosure are welcome.

## Enforcement

We will take reports of violations seriously.

Depending on the circumstances, actions may include:

1. A private warning or request to stop the behavior.
2. Removal or moderation of inappropriate content.
3. Temporary restrictions on participation.
4. Removal from a project or community.
5. Permanent bans from CodexClava spaces or projects.
6. Reporting serious threats, illegal activity, or other matters to the appropriate authorities when necessary.

The response will depend on the severity, context, history, and impact of the behavior.

## Reporting

If you experience or witness behavior that violates this Code of Conduct, please report it privately to the CodexClava maintainers through the contact or reporting channel provided by the relevant project.

When reporting an incident, please include:

* What happened.
* When and where it happened.
* Who was involved, if known.
* Any relevant links, screenshots, or other evidence.
* Any additional context that may help us understand the situation.

Please do not publicly expose personal information about the people involved.

Reports will be handled as confidentially as reasonably possible.

## False or Malicious Reports

Reports should be made in good faith.

Knowingly submitting fabricated evidence, delibrately making false accusations, or using the reporting process to harass or retaliate against another person is itself a violation of this Code of Conduct.

A report that turns out to be mistaken is **not** automatically a bad faith report. People should be able to raise concerns without fear of punishment fo honestly misunderstanding a situation.

## Scope

This Code of Conduct applies to CodexClava's official repositories, discussions, communication channels, events, and other spaces where someone is representing or participating in the CodexClava community.

It may also apply to conduct outside these spaces when that conduct directly affects the safety or ability of people to participate in CodexClava.

## The Standards We Aim For

We don't expect perfection.

We expect people to:

**Build boldly.**<br>
**Learn openly.**<br>
**Disagree honestly.**<br>
**Give credit.**<br>
**Own mistakes.**<br>
**Help others grow.**<br>
**And leave the community stronger than they found it.**

CodexClava exists to build things that don't exist yet. But the kind of community we build along the way matters just as much as what we build.
